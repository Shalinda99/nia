===== VITAL SIGNS — NORMAL RANGES AND CRITICAL VALUES =====

BLOOD PRESSURE:
- Normal: 90-120 / 60-80 mmHg
- Stage 1 HTN: 130-139 / 80-89 mmHg
- Stage 2 HTN: ≥140 / ≥90 mmHg
- Hypertensive Crisis: >180 / >120 mmHg — NOTIFY PHYSICIAN IMMEDIATELY
- Hypotension: SBP <90 mmHg or MAP <65 mmHg — NOTIFY PHYSICIAN
- Orthostatic hypotension: Drop ≥20 mmHg systolic or ≥10 diastolic within 3 minutes of standing

HEART RATE:
- Normal: 60-100 bpm
- Bradycardia: <60 bpm — if symptomatic (dizziness, hypotension) NOTIFY PHYSICIAN
- Tachycardia: >100 bpm — assess cause (fever, pain, volume depletion, arrhythmia)
- CRITICAL: <40 or >150 — NOTIFY PHYSICIAN IMMEDIATELY

RESPIRATORY RATE:
- Normal: 12-20 breaths/min
- Bradypnea: <10 — risk of respiratory failure, NOTIFY PHYSICIAN
- Tachypnea: >20 — assess for pneumonia, pain, metabolic acidosis, PE
- CRITICAL: <8 or >28 — ACTIVATE RAPID RESPONSE

TEMPERATURE:
- Normal: 36.1-37.2°C (97-99°F)
- Low-grade fever: 37.2-38°C (99-100.4°F)
- Fever: >38°C (>100.4°F) — blood cultures, assess for infection source
- Hyperthermia: >40°C (>104°F) — aggressive cooling, NOTIFY PHYSICIAN
- Hypothermia: <36°C (<96.8°F) — active warming, assess for sepsis/exposure

OXYGEN SATURATION (SpO2):
- Normal: 95-100%
- Acceptable in COPD: 88-92% (avoid over-oxygenation — hypercapnia risk)
- Low: 90-94% — increase O2, assess cause
- CRITICAL: <88% — NOTIFY PHYSICIAN, prepare for respiratory support
- Target: ≥95% (most patients) or 88-92% (COPD/chronic hypercapnia)

===== INTRAVENOUS ACCESS AND MANAGEMENT =====

PERIPHERAL IV SITE SELECTION:
- Preferred sites: Forearm (cephalic vein, basilic vein), antecubital fossa
- Avoid: Dominant hand if possible, areas of infection, near joints (elbow), lymphedema arm, AV fistula arm, mastectomy side
- Gauge selection: 18G (blood products, rapid infusion), 20G (most medications), 22G (elderly/difficult veins)

PERIPHERAL IV INSERTION:
1. Gather supplies: IV catheter, tourniquet, alcohol/chlorhexidine swab, transparent dressing, tape
2. Verify physician order; explain procedure to patient
3. Perform hand hygiene, don gloves
4. Apply tourniquet 3-4 inches above intended site
5. Prep skin with alcohol for 30 seconds, allow to dry
6. Stabilize vein, insert at 10-30 degree angle bevel up
7. Advance catheter, release tourniquet, connect extension set
8. Flush with 10mL NS (PUSH-PAUSE technique)
9. Apply transparent dressing, document date/time/gauge/site
10. Document in MAR

IV CATHETER CARE AND MAINTENANCE:
- Change dressing: Every 5-7 days (transparent) or when soiled/loose
- Change tubing: Q96 hours for standard tubing; Q24H for blood products or lipids; Q12H for propofol
- CHANGE SITE: Every 72-96 hours OR upon signs of complications
- Assess every shift: Redness, swelling, pain, infiltration, phlebitis

SIGNS OF IV COMPLICATIONS:
- Infiltration: Swelling, cool, pale skin around site — REMOVE IV
- Extravasation: Infiltration of vesicant drug (chemotherapy, vasopressors) — STOP infusion, follow extravasation protocol, NOTIFY PHYSICIAN
- Phlebitis: Redness, warmth, pain along vein — REMOVE IV, apply warm compress
- Infection: Redness, warmth, purulent drainage — REMOVE IV, culture site, notify physician

CENTRAL LINE CARE (CLABSI Prevention Bundle):
1. Hand hygiene before any central line access
2. Maximal sterile barrier during insertion (full drape, gown, gloves, mask, cap)
3. Chlorhexidine skin prep (allow to dry 30 seconds)
4. Optimal site selection: Subclavian preferred > jugular > femoral
5. Daily necessity review — remove line when no longer needed
6. Dressing change: Q7 days (transparent) or Q2 days (gauze) or when soiled/loose
7. Scrub the hub x15 seconds before accessing
8. Disinfection cap on all ports when not in use

===== BLOOD GLUCOSE MONITORING AND INSULIN ADMINISTRATION =====

BLOOD GLUCOSE TARGET RANGES:
- General hospital patients (non-ICU): 140-180 mg/dL
- Critically ill (ICU): 140-180 mg/dL (avoid <70 and >200)
- Cardiac surgery: 140-180 mg/dL
- Pregnancy: 60-95 mg/dL (fasting), <140 mg/dL (1hr postprandial)

HYPOGLYCEMIA PROTOCOL (BG <70 mg/dL):
1. If conscious and able to swallow: 15-20g fast-acting carbohydrates
   - 4oz (120mL) apple juice or orange juice
   - 3-4 glucose tablets (4g each)
   - 8oz regular soda (not diet)
2. If NPO or unable to take PO: D50W 25mL IV push (provides 12.5g dextrose)
3. Recheck blood glucose in 15 minutes
4. Repeat treatment if still <70 mg/dL
5. Once BG >100 mg/dL, ensure patient has meal or snack
6. If BG <50 mg/dL (severe): D50W 50mL IV push, notify physician
7. Document: Pre-treatment BG, treatment given, post-treatment BG
8. Reassess BG every 30-60 min x2 after hypoglycemia

HYPERGLYCEMIA CORRECTION:
- Follow sliding scale insulin order (physician-ordered)
- Notify physician if BG >250 mg/dL not responding to sliding scale
- Check for ketones if BG >300 mg/dL (DKA screening)

===== FOLEY CATHETER CARE (CAUTI Prevention) =====

INDICATIONS (use only when necessary):
- Urinary retention
- Accurate urinary output measurement (hemodynamically unstable)
- Operative procedures
- Wound management (stage 3/4 pressure injury in incontinent patient)
- Palliative care/comfort

INSERTION TECHNIQUE (sterile):
1. Assess for allergies (latex, betadine)
2. Sterile field and supplies (sterile gloves, drapes, catheter, collection bag)
3. Clean per-urethral area with betadine or chlorhexidine (3x downward strokes for female)
4. Lubricate catheter tip
5. Insert catheter until urine flows (additional 2-3cm after urine flow in male)
6. Inflate balloon with sterile water per manufacturer (typically 10mL)
7. Secure catheter to thigh (prevents trauma)
8. Ensure collection bag is BELOW bladder level at all times

DAILY MAINTENANCE (CAUTI Prevention Bundle):
1. Perform hand hygiene before and after any catheter manipulation
2. Daily pericare with soap and water (do not use antiseptic routinely)
3. Maintain closed drainage system — do NOT disconnect catheter from drainage bag
4. Secure catheter to prevent traction
5. Keep collection bag below bladder level — never rest on floor
6. Empty drainage bag when 2/3 full or every shift — use separate container per patient
7. Assess daily necessity — REMOVE when no longer indicated
8. Document daily: Urine output, color, clarity, odor

===== WOUND CARE AND DRESSING CHANGES =====

WOUND ASSESSMENT (MEASURE):
- M — Measure: Length x width x depth (cm)
- E — Exudate: Amount (none, minimal, moderate, heavy) and type (serous, serosanguineous, purulent)
- A — Appearance: Wound bed (granulation, slough, eschar, necrosis)
- S — Surrounding skin: Color, warmth, edema, maceration
- U — Undermining: Depth and direction of undermining/tunneling
- R — Reassess: Compare to previous assessment
- E — Edge: Attached, rolled, undermined

WOUND TYPES AND DRESSINGS:
- Clean/healing wound: Transparent film or simple gauze
- Wounds with minimal exudate: Hydrocolloid (DuoDerm)
- Wounds with moderate exudate: Foam dressing
- Infected/heavily exudating: Calcium alginate, silver dressings
- Wounds with slough: Enzymatic debrider, hydrogel
- Tunneling/deep wounds: Packing with saline-moistened gauze or iodoform

SURGICAL WOUND CARE:
- Keep dry for first 24-48 hours (per surgeon)
- Then: Clean with NS or mild soap and water
- Remove dressing: Wet-to-dry NOT recommended (damages granulation tissue)
- Assess for REEDA: Redness, Edema, Ecchymosis, Drainage, Approximation
- Signs of infection: Increasing redness, warmth, pain, purulent discharge, fever
- Staple/suture removal: Per surgeon order (typically 7-14 days depending on site)

===== NASOGASTRIC TUBE (NGT) CARE =====

CONFIRMING NG TUBE PLACEMENT:
- GOLD STANDARD: Chest X-ray confirmation (required for initial placement and if dislodged)
- Auscultation alone NOT reliable for confirmation
- pH testing of aspirate: <5 suggests gastric placement
- Mark tube at nose insertion point with tape
- DO NOT use tube until radiographically confirmed

NG TUBE MANAGEMENT:
- Assess placement before EACH feeding/medication administration
- Reconfirm if: Patient coughs/vomits, tube moves at marking, respiratory distress
- Continuous feeds: Check residual Q4H (hold if >250-500mL per facility policy)
- Keep HOB at 30-45° during and for 1 hour after feeding
- Flush with 30mL water after medications and Q4H during continuous feeds
- Reposition tube taping daily to prevent pressure injury

===== SBAR COMMUNICATION TOOL =====

SITUATION:
"I am [name], nurse caring for [patient name] in room [number]. I'm calling about [specific concern]. The patient appears [acute/stable/deteriorating]."

BACKGROUND:
"The patient is a [age]-year-old [male/female] with a history of [relevant diagnoses], admitted [date] for [admission diagnosis]. Relevant medications include [list]."

ASSESSMENT:
"I believe the problem is [your assessment]. The patient's vital signs are [list]. Current status: [mental status, respiratory status, etc.]."

RECOMMENDATION:
"I need you to [specific request — come see patient, order labs, change medication, transfer]. I also recommend [additional steps]."

EXAMPLE:
S: "Dr. Johnson, this is Nurse Taylor calling about patient John Smith in room 204A. He's acutely short of breath."
B: "Mr. Smith is a 67-year-old with CHF admitted 3 days ago. He was improving but in the last hour his breathing has worsened."
A: "His SpO2 dropped to 88% on 2L NC. RR is 26. BP 158/96. I think he may be in acute pulmonary edema."
R: "I need you to come assess him now. I've increased O2 to 4L. Should I administer his PRN furosemide?"
