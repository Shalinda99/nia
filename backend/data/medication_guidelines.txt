===== COMMON CARDIAC MEDICATIONS =====

FUROSEMIDE (Lasix)
- Class: Loop diuretic
- Mechanism: Inhibits Na-K-2Cl transporter in loop of Henle
- Indications: CHF, edema, pulmonary edema, hypertension, hypercalcemia
- Doses: PO: 20-80mg QD-BID; IV: 20-80mg (max 600mg/day); IV drip: 0.1-1mg/kg/hr
- IV push rate: ≤4 mg/min (max 40mg/min); high doses ≤0.5mg/min (ototoxicity risk)
- Monitor: Electrolytes (K, Na, Mg), BUN/Cr, BMP, urine output, weight
- Side effects: Hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hyponatremia, ototoxicity, dehydration, increased creatinine
- Contraindications: Anuria, sulfonamide allergy, hepatic coma
- Nursing considerations: Monitor I&O, daily weights, electrolyte replacement as ordered

METOPROLOL (Lopressor/Toprol XL)
- Class: Beta-1 selective beta-blocker
- Indications: Hypertension, angina, CHF, tachyarrhythmias, post-MI
- Doses: PO immediate-release: 25-100mg BID; Extended-release: 25-200mg daily; IV: 5mg Q5min x3 (max 15mg for arrhythmia)
- Monitor: Heart rate (hold if HR <50), BP (hold if SBP <90), blood glucose (may mask hypoglycemia signs)
- Side effects: Bradycardia, hypotension, fatigue, bronchospasm, depression, erectile dysfunction
- Contraindications: Severe bradycardia, 2nd/3rd degree AV block, severe bronchospasm, cardiogenic shock
- Do not abruptly discontinue — taper over 1-2 weeks

LISINOPRIL (Prinivil/Zestril)
- Class: ACE Inhibitor
- Indications: Hypertension, CHF, post-MI, diabetic nephropathy
- Doses: Hypertension: 10-40mg daily; CHF: 5-40mg daily; Start low in CKD
- Monitor: BP, renal function (K, Cr), presence of cough
- Side effects: Dry cough (10-15%), hyperkalemia, angioedema (STOP immediately if angioedema), hypotension (first dose), increased creatinine
- Contraindications: Pregnancy (teratogenic!), bilateral renal artery stenosis, history of ACE-inhibitor angioedema, concurrent with aliskiren in diabetes
- First-dose hypotension: Give at bedtime; ensure hydration

APIXABAN (Eliquis)
- Class: Direct Xa inhibitor (DOAC)
- Indications: Afib stroke prevention, DVT/PE treatment and prevention
- Doses: Afib: 5mg BID (2.5mg BID if ≥2 of: age ≥80, weight ≤60kg, Cr ≥1.5)
- DVT/PE treatment: 10mg BID x7 days, then 5mg BID
- Monitor: Signs of bleeding (GI, intracranial, urinary), hemoglobin
- Side effects: Bleeding, bruising, anemia
- Reversal agent: Andexxa (andexanet alfa) for life-threatening bleeding
- Do NOT crush tablets
- Hold 24-48 hours before invasive procedures

WARFARIN (Coumadin)
- Class: Vitamin K antagonist
- Indications: Afib, mechanical heart valve, DVT/PE, hypercoagulable states
- Doses: Highly individualized based on INR — typical 2-10mg daily
- Therapeutic INR: 2-3 for most indications; 2.5-3.5 for mechanical mitral valve
- Monitor: INR (daily in hospital, weekly to monthly when stable), signs of bleeding
- Many drug and food interactions (warfarin + antibiotics, warfarin + amiodarone = significantly increased INR)
- Foods high in Vitamin K decrease effect: leafy greens, broccoli, kale
- Reversal: Vitamin K (slow) 2.5-10mg PO/IV; FFP/4-factor PCC for urgent reversal

HEPARIN (Unfractionated)
- Class: Anticoagulant
- Indications: DVT/PE treatment, ACS, CABG, dialysis, PE prevention
- DVT prophylaxis: 5000 units SubQ Q8-12H
- Therapeutic: Weight-based protocol; target aPTT 60-100 seconds
- CRITICAL: Must double-check dose with second nurse (high-alert medication)
- Monitor: aPTT, anti-Xa level, CBC for HIT (heparin-induced thrombocytopenia), signs of bleeding
- Reversal: Protamine sulfate (1mg per 100 units heparin given in last 2-3 hours)
- HIT: Platelet drop >50% from baseline — STOP heparin immediately, notify physician

===== COMMON ANTIBIOTICS =====

VANCOMYCIN
- Class: Glycopeptide antibiotic
- Indications: MRSA, C. diff (PO only), gram-positive infections, endocarditis
- Dose: 15-20 mg/kg Q8-12H (obese: dose by actual body weight, extend interval); typically 1-1.5g Q12H
- Target: Trough 10-20 mcg/mL OR AUC/MIC 400-600 (AUC-guided preferred)
- Monitor: Kidney function (Cr, BUN), trough levels, audiometry if prolonged use
- Side effects: Red man syndrome (histamine release with rapid infusion — SLOW infusion!), nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity
- INFUSE SLOWLY: ≤10-15mg/min; if red man syndrome occurs — slow rate, diphenhydramine
- Adjust dose for renal impairment

MEROPENEM
- Class: Carbapenem (broad-spectrum beta-lactam)
- Indications: Severe infections, ESBL organisms, pseudomonas, polymicrobial infections
- Dose: 1g IV Q8H (standard); 2g IV Q8H for CNS infections or pseudomonas
- Monitor: Renal function, seizures (lowers seizure threshold), CBC
- Side effects: Diarrhea, nausea, headache, C. diff risk with prolonged use
- Carbapenem-resistant organisms (CRE) are emerging — culture and sensitivity critical

PIPERACILLIN-TAZOBACTAM (Zosyn)
- Class: Extended-spectrum penicillin + beta-lactamase inhibitor
- Indications: Intra-abdominal infections, hospital-acquired pneumonia, UTI, polymicrobial infections
- Dose: 3.375g IV Q6H or 4.5g IV Q8H; extended infusion 3.375g over 4hrs Q8H for severe
- Monitor: Renal function, CBC (may cause neutropenia with prolonged use), electrolytes (contains sodium)
- Allergy caution: Cross-reactivity with penicillin allergy (low risk ~1-2%)

CEFTRIAXONE (Rocephin)
- Class: 3rd-generation cephalosporin
- Indications: Community-acquired pneumonia, meningitis, gonorrhea, UTI, Lyme disease
- Dose: 1-2g IV/IM daily; 2g Q12H for meningitis
- Monitor: Renal function, LFTs (biliary sludge with prolonged use in neonates)
- Side effects: Diarrhea, rash, C. diff risk
- Do NOT mix with calcium-containing solutions (Ringer's) — precipitates

AZITHROMYCIN (Zithromax)
- Class: Macrolide antibiotic
- Indications: Community-acquired pneumonia (atypicals), COPD exacerbation, STIs, MAC prophylaxis
- Dose: 500mg PO/IV daily (500mg x1, then 250mg x4 for PAC)
- Monitor: QTc prolongation (avoid with other QT-prolonging drugs, hypokalemia), LFTs
- Drug interactions: Warfarin (increases INR), QT-prolonging medications

===== INSULIN TYPES AND ADMINISTRATION =====

RAPID-ACTING INSULIN:
- Aspart (NovoLog), Lispro (Humalog), Glulisine (Apidra)
- Onset: 10-20 min | Peak: 1-3 hours | Duration: 3-5 hours
- Use: Mealtime dosing, sliding scale, correction doses
- Give SubQ 15 minutes before meals OR IV infusion in ICU
- CRITICAL: Store in refrigerator; do not freeze; clear/colorless

SHORT-ACTING INSULIN:
- Regular insulin (Humulin R, Novolin R)
- Onset: 30-60 min | Peak: 2-5 hours | Duration: 5-8 hours
- IV use: Only regular insulin can be given IV
- SubQ: Give 30 minutes before meals

INTERMEDIATE-ACTING:
- NPH (Humulin N, Novolin N)
- Cloudy appearance — ROLL, do not shake
- Onset: 1-3 hrs | Peak: 6-12 hrs | Duration: 16-24 hrs

LONG-ACTING INSULIN:
- Glargine (Lantus, Basaglar), Detemir (Levemir), Degludec (Tresiba)
- Onset: 2-4 hrs | No peak | Duration: 20-24+ hours
- Once daily dosing (Lantus/Basaglar: QHS or same time daily)
- CLEAR solution — do NOT mix with other insulins
- Do NOT administer IV

INSULIN SAFETY:
- High-alert medication — REQUIRES double-nurse verification
- ALWAYS check blood glucose before administering
- Document blood glucose, dose, and site
- Rotate injection sites (abdomen absorbs fastest)
- Hypoglycemia protocol: BG <70 mg/dL — give 15g fast carbs (4oz juice, glucose tabs), recheck in 15 min

===== VASOPRESSORS =====

NOREPINEPHRINE (Levophed)
- First-line vasopressor for septic shock
- Mechanism: Alpha-1 > Beta-1 adrenergic agonist (vasoconstriction + inotropy)
- Dose: 0.01-3 mcg/kg/min IV infusion
- Target: MAP ≥65 mmHg
- Requires central venous access (peripheral only in emergency)
- Monitor: MAP (arterial line preferred), HR, end-organ perfusion, skin color/temperature
- Side effects: Peripheral vasoconstriction, digital ischemia, tachyarrhythmias, hypertension

VASOPRESSIN
- Add-on to norepinephrine for septic shock
- Dose: Fixed rate 0.03 units/min (NOT titrated based on response)
- Lowers norepinephrine dose requirements
- Use for vasopressor-refractory shock

DOPAMINE
- Second-line vasopressor (more arrhythmogenic than norepinephrine)
- Low dose (1-5 mcg/kg/min): Dopaminergic — renal/splanchnic dilation
- Medium dose (5-10 mcg/kg/min): Beta-1 — increased CO and HR
- High dose (>10 mcg/kg/min): Alpha-1 — vasoconstriction

===== RESPIRATORY MEDICATIONS =====

ALBUTEROL (Ventolin, ProAir)
- Class: Short-acting beta-2 agonist (SABA)
- Indications: Bronchospasm, asthma, COPD exacerbation
- Nebulizer dose: 2.5mg in 3mL NS Q4-6H or Q20min for severe
- MDI: 2 puffs Q4-6H PRN
- IV: For severe cases only (0.1-0.2 mg/kg/hr)
- Side effects: Tachycardia, hypokalemia, tremor, palpitations
- Monitor: Heart rate, potassium, respiratory status

IPRATROPIUM (Atrovent)
- Class: Short-acting muscarinic antagonist (SAMA)
- Indications: COPD, bronchospasm (synergistic with albuterol)
- Nebulizer: 0.5mg Q4-6H
- Often combined with albuterol (DuoNeb)
- Side effects: Dry mouth, urinary retention, constipation, blurred vision
- Caution: Glaucoma (avoid nebulizer in eyes)

METHYLPREDNISOLONE (Solu-Medrol)
- Class: Systemic corticosteroid
- Indications: COPD exacerbation, asthma, allergic reactions, inflammation
- COPD exacerbation: 40mg IV Q12H x 5 days (or prednisolone 40mg PO)
- Asthma exacerbation: 1-2 mg/kg IV Q6H (max 125mg)
- Side effects: Hyperglycemia (monitor blood glucose!), hypertension, insomnia, immunosuppression, adrenal suppression
- Monitor: Blood glucose every 6 hours during treatment, BP
