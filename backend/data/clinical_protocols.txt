===== SEPSIS AND SEPTIC SHOCK PROTOCOL (SURVIVING SEPSIS CAMPAIGN) =====

DEFINITION:
- Sepsis: Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by dysregulated host response to infection.
- SOFA Score >2 points from baseline indicates organ dysfunction.
- Septic Shock: Sepsis + vasopressor requirement to maintain MAP ≥65 mmHg + serum lactate >2 mmol/L despite adequate fluid resuscitation.

RECOGNITION — qSOFA Score (2 or more = high risk for sepsis):
1. Respiratory rate ≥22 breaths/min (1 point)
2. Altered mentation / GCS <15 (1 point)
3. Systolic BP ≤100 mmHg (1 point)

SEPSIS 1-HOUR BUNDLE (HOUR-1 BUNDLE):
1. Measure lactate level — resuscitate if lactate ≥4 mmol/L
2. Obtain blood cultures BEFORE antibiotics (minimum 2 sets from 2 sites)
3. Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour
4. Begin rapid 30 mL/kg IV crystalloid bolus (for hypotension or lactate ≥4)
5. Apply vasopressors if hypotensive during or after fluid resuscitation — target MAP ≥65 mmHg

ANTIBIOTIC EMPIRIC THERAPY:
- Community-acquired: Piperacillin-tazobactam 3.375g IV Q6H or Ceftriaxone 2g IV daily + Azithromycin
- Hospital-acquired / HCAP: Vancomycin 25 mg/kg IV + Piperacillin-tazobactam OR Meropenem
- Known ESBL: Meropenem 1g IV Q8H
- Neutropenic fever: Piperacillin-tazobactam + Vancomycin

FLUID RESUSCITATION:
- Use balanced crystalloids (Lactated Ringer's preferred over NS for large volumes)
- 30 mL/kg IV fluid bolus for hypotension or lactate ≥4 mmol/L
- Reassess after each bolus with passive leg raise or fluid responsiveness testing
- Avoid over-resuscitation — monitor for pulmonary edema

VASOPRESSORS:
- First-line: Norepinephrine (Levophed) — target MAP ≥65 mmHg
- Add Vasopressin 0.03 units/min if norepinephrine >0.25 mcg/kg/min (to spare norepinephrine)
- Add Epinephrine or Dopamine as second vasopressor if needed
- Consider Hydrocortisone 200mg/day IV if vasopressor-refractory shock

MONITORING:
- Lactate trending (repeat at 2 hours — should be decreasing)
- UO target ≥0.5 mL/kg/hr
- Continuous cardiac monitoring, SpO2
- Blood cultures x2 sets before antibiotics
- CBC, BMP, LFTs, coagulation studies, procalcitonin, CRP

SOURCE CONTROL:
- Identify and remove source within 6-12 hours (drain abscess, remove infected device)

NURSING ACTIONS:
- Initiate sepsis alert protocol immediately
- Large-bore IV access x2 or central venous access
- Foley catheter for strict I&O
- Head of bed 30-45 degrees
- Frequent vital sign monitoring (q15-30 min during active resuscitation)
- Notify physician of any deterioration
- Document fluid administered and response

===== FALL PREVENTION PROTOCOL =====

FALL RISK ASSESSMENT (Morse Fall Scale):
- History of falls within 3 months: Yes=25, No=0
- Secondary diagnosis (≥2 medical diagnoses): Yes=15, No=0
- Ambulatory aid: None/bedrest/wheelchair=0, Crutches/cane/walker=15, Furniture=30
- IV or heparin lock: Yes=20, No=0
- Gait: Normal/bedrest=0, Weak=10, Impaired=20
- Mental status: Oriented to own ability=0, Forgets limitations=15

Score 0-24: Low Risk | 25-44: Medium Risk | 45+: High Risk

FALL PREVENTION INTERVENTIONS (High Risk):
1. Bed in lowest position at all times
2. Bed alarm activated at all times
3. Non-skid footwear in place when OOB
4. Call light within reach at all times
5. Hourly rounding — 4 P's (Pain, Position, Potty, Possessions)
6. Yellow fall risk band on patient wristband
7. Yellow fall risk sign on patient door
8. Side rails up x3
9. Personal items within easy reach
10. Clear pathway to bathroom
11. Adequate lighting (nightlight)
12. Medications review — sedatives, diuretics, antihypertensives increase risk
13. Ambulate with assist x2 for high-risk patients
14. Consider physical therapy consult

POST-FALL PROTOCOL:
1. Ensure patient safety — do NOT move if head/neck injury suspected
2. Call for help immediately
3. Assess for injury (head, extremities, back)
4. Notify charge nurse and attending physician immediately
5. Document neurological assessment (LOC, pupils, orientation)
6. Vital signs immediately and q15 min x 1hr
7. Complete incident report within 1 hour
8. Reassess fall risk score
9. Update care plan
10. Notify family
11. CT scan of head if anticoagulated or any neurological change

===== PRESSURE INJURY PREVENTION PROTOCOL (NPUAP Staging) =====

BRADEN SCALE RISK FACTORS:
- Sensory perception (1-4): Ability to respond to pressure-related discomfort
- Moisture (1-4): Degree to which skin is exposed to moisture
- Activity (1-4): Degree of physical activity
- Mobility (1-4): Ability to change and control body position
- Nutrition (1-4): Usual food intake pattern
- Friction/Shear (1-3): Assistance needed to move

Score ≤18 = At Risk | ≤12 = High Risk | ≤9 = Very High Risk

PRESSURE INJURY STAGING:
- Stage 1: Non-blanchable erythema, intact skin
- Stage 2: Partial thickness skin loss, exposed dermis, blister
- Stage 3: Full thickness tissue loss, subcutaneous fat may be visible
- Stage 4: Full thickness tissue/muscle/bone loss
- Unstageable: Obscured by slough/eschar
- DTPI (Deep Tissue Pressure Injury): Persistent non-blanchable deep red, maroon, or purple

PREVENTION INTERVENTIONS:
1. Reposition every 2 hours (q2h) and document
2. Use heel protectors / float heels off bed
3. Moisture barrier cream for incontinent patients
4. Specialty pressure-relieving mattress for high-risk
5. Nutrition consult for malnutrition risk
6. Keep skin clean and dry
7. Nutritional supplementation (Vitamin C, Zinc, Protein)
8. Assess and document skin at every shift
9. Do not massage reddened areas

===== CODE BLUE / RAPID RESPONSE TEAM (RRT) PROTOCOL =====

ACTIVATE RAPID RESPONSE (RRT) FOR:
- Acute change in mental status
- Respiratory rate <8 or >28 breaths/min
- SpO2 <90% despite supplemental O2
- Systolic BP <90 mmHg or MAP <65 mmHg
- Heart rate <40 or >130 bpm
- Acute chest pain
- Nurse concern about patient status — "If in doubt, call it out"

CODE BLUE INDICATORS:
- Cardiac arrest (no pulse, no breathing)
- Respiratory arrest
- Pulseless ventricular tachycardia or ventricular fibrillation

CODE BLUE RESPONSE:
1. Call code: Press code button OR dial CODE BLUE on phone + state location
2. Start CPR immediately (30:2 ratio, hard and fast, 100-120/min)
3. Get AED/defibrillator to bedside
4. Establish IV access
5. Assign roles: Team leader, compressor, airway, IV, recorder
6. Follow ACLS algorithm
7. Document time of arrest, interventions, medications

BLS SEQUENCE:
1. Check scene safety
2. Check responsiveness (tap and shout)
3. Call for help / activate code
4. Check pulse (≤10 seconds) and breathing
5. If no pulse: Begin CPR — 30 compressions, 2 breaths
6. AED as soon as available — turn on, follow prompts
7. Continue CPR until AED charged, minimize interruptions

===== PAIN ASSESSMENT AND MANAGEMENT PROTOCOL =====

ASSESSMENT TOOLS:
- Numeric Rating Scale (NRS): 0-10 (use for alert, cooperative patients)
  - 0: No pain | 1-3: Mild | 4-6: Moderate | 7-10: Severe
- CPOT (Critical Care Pain Observation Tool): For intubated/sedated patients
  - Facial expression, body movements, muscle tension, compliance with ventilator
  - Score 0-8 (>2 indicates significant pain)
- FLACC Scale: For pediatric/cognitively impaired patients

REASSESSMENT INTERVALS:
- After IV pain medication: 30 minutes
- After PO/IM pain medication: 1 hour
- Stable pain (NRS ≤3): Every 4 hours
- Unstable/uncontrolled pain: Every 1-2 hours

PAIN MANAGEMENT LADDER:
1. Non-pharmacological: Positioning, ice/heat, distraction, music therapy
2. Non-opioid: Acetaminophen (Tylenol) 325-1000mg PO/IV Q4-6H (max 4g/day), NSAIDs (if no contraindications)
3. Mild-moderate opioid: Tramadol 50-100mg PO Q4-6H, Oxycodone 5-10mg PO Q4-6H
4. Moderate-severe opioid: Morphine 2-4mg IV Q4H PRN, Hydromorphone 0.2-0.4mg IV Q3-4H
5. PCA (Patient Controlled Analgesia): For post-op or oncology patients

OPIOID SAFETY:
- Monitor respiratory rate and sedation level
- Naloxone (Narcan) at bedside for opioid patients
- Naloxone dose: 0.4mg IV/IM for respiratory depression (RR <8, sedation level >2)
- Do not administer opioids with RR <10 or sedation score ≥3

===== HAND HYGIENE AND INFECTION CONTROL =====

5 MOMENTS OF HAND HYGIENE (WHO):
1. Before touching patient
2. Before clean/aseptic procedure
3. After body fluid exposure/risk
4. After touching patient
5. After touching patient surroundings

PROPER TECHNIQUE:
- Alcohol-based hand rub (ABHR): 20-30 seconds — preferred unless hands visibly soiled or C. diff exposure
- Soap and water: 40-60 seconds — required for visible soiling, C. diff, Norovirus

ISOLATION PRECAUTIONS:
- Standard Precautions: ALL patients — gloves, hand hygiene, care with sharps
- Droplet Precautions: Influenza, COVID-19, pertussis — surgical mask, eye protection
- Airborne Precautions: TB, measles, varicella — N95 respirator, negative pressure room
- Contact Precautions: MRSA, VRE, C. diff, ESBL — gown + gloves for ALL contact

===== MEDICATION ADMINISTRATION SAFETY (FIVE RIGHTS + THREE CHECKS) =====

FIVE RIGHTS:
1. Right Patient — scan wristband, verify name and DOB verbally
2. Right Drug — verify against MAR and physician order
3. Right Dose — calculate and double-check (have second nurse verify high-alert meds)
4. Right Route — verify appropriate route, IV patency
5. Right Time — within 30 minutes of scheduled time (1 hour for PRN)

THREE CHECKS:
1. When pulling medication from drawer/storage
2. When preparing medication
3. At the bedside before administering

HIGH-ALERT MEDICATIONS (require 2-nurse verification):
- Insulin (all types)
- Heparin (IV infusions and subQ)
- Concentrated electrolytes (KCl, NaCl 3%, MgSO4)
- Chemotherapy agents
- Neuromuscular blocking agents
- Anticoagulants (Warfarin, Lovenox, direct oral anticoagulants)
- Opioid infusions (morphine, hydromorphone, fentanyl)
- Vasoactive medications (norepinephrine, epinephrine, vasopressin)

NEVER EVENTS — MEDICATION:
- Never administer medications to which patient has documented allergy
- Never give IV potassium undiluted or rapid push
- Never abbreviate "U" for units (write "units")
- Never crush extended-release or enteric-coated medications
